## 0. The new definitions

### `IsEuclideanDiffusion V Pt` / `IsEuclideanLangevin V Pt`
`(∀ t, Markov (Pt t))`, `Pt (s+t) = Pt t ∘ₖ Pt s` (Mathlib `(η ∘ₖ κ) x = (κ x).bind η`, so first `Pt s` then `Pt t`: correct Chapman-Kolmogorov), and Dynkin/Kolmogorov identity `Pt_t f(x) = f(x) + ∫_0^t P_s(𝓛f)(x) ds` for `f ∈ C_c^∞`, `𝓛f = Δf - ⟨∇V,∇f⟩`; `IsEuclideanLangevin` adds `V ∈ C^∞` and `gibbs V` a probability measure.
- Generator is right: `generator V f = laplacian f - ⟨∇V, ∇f⟩`, i.e. `Δ - ∇V·∇`, the generator of `dX = -∇V dt + √2 dB`.
- Consequences of the axioms: the identity at `t = 0` gives `∫ f dP_0 x = f x` for all `f ∈ C_c^∞`, so `P_0 x = δ_x`. Markov (probability kernels) means no explosion; for the convex `V` of every statement below (all carry `Hess V ≥ ρ ≥ 0`, or `V` quadratic) the SDE is non-explosive, so no solution is excluded.
- Junk check on the Dynkin integral: `∫ s in 0..t` is `0` when `s ↦ P_s(𝓛f)(x)` is not interval-integrable, and no measurability in `s` of `t ↦ Pt t` is assumed. This cannot be exploited: `isEuclideanDiffusion_unique` (proved, `Extra/EuclideanDiffusion.lean`) shows that any `Pt` satisfying the three axioms for `C^∞` `V` with probability Gibbs measure is the canonical kernel (equal to the Brownian `langevinKernel` for convex `V`, `isEuclideanDiffusion_eq_langevinKernel`). So `IsEuclideanLangevin V Pt` determines `Pt` uniquely and `Pt t x = Law(X_t^x)`; the hypothesis is equivalent to "`Pt` is the transition law of the SDE solution". Also `Measure.bind` and `Measure.map` are applied only to measurable kernels, so the old junk (`P.map` of a non-measurable `X x t`, `ν.bind` of a non-measurable map) is gone: this is an improvement over the old statements.
- Non-vacuity: `exists_euclideanLangevin` (smooth convex `V`, probability Gibbs) and `exists_euclideanLangevin_of_smooth` (no convexity). Scratch check (`auditL.lean`): for `V = gaussPot d ρ = ρ‖x‖²/2 + (d/2) log(2π/ρ)` (`d > 0`, `ρ > 0`), `∃ Pt, IsEuclideanLangevin V Pt ∧ RigidCurvature V` is proved (`spectralGap V = ρ` from `ou_first_eigenspace`, Hessian `ρ I`). So the hypotheses of `eigenmap_is_ou` (together with `T = (√ρ x_i)` from `ou_first_eigenspace`), of `ou_fisher_information`, `ou_mean_start_cutoff` (take `c n = (n/2) log(2π/ρ)`, `n > 0`), `ou_lp_infty_fails` and of the `IsRigidSequenceK` statements (take `V n = gaussPot n ρ`, `liminf λ₁ = ρ > 0`) are jointly satisfiable; they are not contradictory, and the conclusions are not trivial (they are the cutoff statements for the explicit OU model).
- Deviation from old `IsLangevin`: `C²` becomes `C^∞` (flagged "Amendment" in each docstring or inside the definition docstring). Severity low: it restricts the class of potentials (manuscript, line 92: `V` strictly convex and `C²`); needed because uniqueness of the semigroup is formalized through the smooth Riemannian bridge. For the quadratic/OU statements it is vacuous (automatic).
- Dropped: `IsStdBrownian`, carrier `(Ω, B, P, X)`. Added: Markov kernels, semigroup law, Dynkin identity. By uniqueness these encode exactly "`Pt t x` = law of the solution at time `t` from `x`", which is all that the statements use (they only use laws `P.map (X x t)`).

### `IsSigmaDiffusion V σ Pt` / `IsSigmaLangevin V σ Pt`
Same three axioms with generator `L_σ f = σ² Δf - ⟨∇V, ∇f⟩`; `IsSigmaLangevin` adds `V ∈ C^∞` and `gibbs (V/σ²)` a probability measure.
- Faithful to `dY = -∇V dt + √(2σ²) dB`: the generator of an SDE `dY = b dt + s dB` is `(s²/2) Δ + b·∇`; with `s = √2 σ`, `b = -∇V` this is `σ²Δ - ∇V·∇`, as in the manuscript (line 849-850: "generator `σ²Δ - ∇V·∇`"). Invariant law `e^{-V/σ²}/Z_σ`: matches the manuscript, since `L_σ = σ²(Δ - ∇(V/σ²)·∇)`. The normalizability of `e^{-V/σ²}` is carried by `IsProbabilityMeasure (gibbs (V/σ²))`, as in the old `hp` (the manuscript normalizes by `Z_σ`; taking `Z_σ = 1` is the same without-loss-of-generality constant shift as for `σ = 1`, flagged in old docstrings).
- Cross-check by the proved time-change lemma `isEuclideanLangevin_of_sigma`: `u ↦ Pt (u/σ²)` is `IsEuclideanLangevin (V/σ²)`; hence `Pt` is unique (`Pt t x = Q(σ² t) x`, `Q` the canonical kernel of `V/σ²`), and equals the law of the SDE solution (manuscript line 858: obtained from Normalization with `α = σ²` and `V/σ²`).
- Non-vacuity: scratch check `IsSigmaDiffusion V 1 Pt ↔ IsEuclideanDiffusion V Pt` (by `simp`), so for `σ = 1` the hypothesis is `IsEuclideanLangevin`, satisfiable by `exists_euclideanLangevin` (e.g. `V = gaussPot d ρ`). For `σ ≠ 1`: `V = σ² · gaussPot d ρ'` gives `V/σ² = gaussPot`, and `Pt t := Q(σ² t)` satisfies the three axioms by the same change of variable that is machine-checked in the forward direction (`isEuclideanLangevin_of_sigma`); I did not formalize this converse (limitation; it is the same `intervalIntegral.integral_comp_mul_left` computation with `σ²` instead of `σ⁻²`). By hand it holds: `d/dt Q(σ²t) f = σ² Q_{σ²t}(𝓛_{V/σ²} f) = Q_{σ²t}(σ²Δf - ∇V·∇f)`.

## 1. `eigenmap_is_ou`: faithful with remarks

Manuscript Lemma 3.3 (lines 910-917): for `T ∈ 𝕊_{F₁}`, `(T(X_t))_t` is a `k₁`-dimensional OU process scaled by `ρ`, i.e. a process on `ℝ^{k₁}` with generator `ρΔ - ρ x·∇`. The proof computes `𝓛(g∘T) = (ρΔg - ρ x·∇g)∘T`, which is a function of `T`, hence `T(X)` is Markov.
- Lean: `∀ x₀ t, (Pt t x₀).map Tv = N(e^{-ρt} Tv x₀, (1 - e^{-2ρt}) I_{k₁})`, with `ρ = spectralGap V`, `Tv x = (T i x)_i`.
- Normalization check: OU `dY = -ρY dt + √(2ρ) dβ` has generator `ρΔ - ρ y·∇`, mean `e^{-ρt}y₀`, variance `2ρ ∫_0^t e^{-2ρ(t-s)} ds = 1 - e^{-2ρt}` per coordinate, stationary law `N(0, I)`. This matches the Lean law and the manuscript's own Mehler formula `Z_t ~ N(T(x₀)e^{-ρt}, (1 - e^{-2ρt}) I_{k₁})` (line ~968) and `T(Y_t) ~ γ_{k₁}` for `Y₀ ~ μ`. Note the Lean covariance has no `1/ρ` (unlike `ou_fisher_information` for the coordinates `x`, where the variance is `(1-e^{-2ρt})/ρ`): consistent, because the rows of `T` have norm `√ρ`, so `Var(f_i(X_t)) = ρ · (1-e^{-2ρt})/ρ`. Good.
- `multivariateGaussian m S` is `stdGaussian` pushed by `m + √S`, covariance `S`; `S = (1 - e^{-2ρt}) I` is PSD for `t ≥ 0` (no `not PosSemidef` → Dirac junk branch); `t = 0` gives `S = 0` and the Dirac mass at `Tv x₀`, which equals `(P_0 x₀).map Tv`. `Tv` is continuous (eigenfunctions are `C²` in `IsEigenfunction`), so the `map` is a genuine pushforward.
- What the law form gives: the one-time marginal from every `x₀`, depending on `x₀` only through `Tv x₀`. Is anything lost relative to "(T(X_t)) is an OU (Markov) process"? Not in the kernel framework. For bounded measurable `g`, the statement says `Pt_s (g∘Tv)(x) = (Q_s g)(Tv x)` for all `x`, `s`, with `Q` the explicit OU semigroup. This is exactly the lumping (Dynkin) criterion: since `X` is Markov with transition kernels `Pt` (semigroup law, and by uniqueness these are the transition kernels of the SDE), `E[g(T X_{t+s}) | F_t] = (Q_s g)(T X_t)`, so `T(X)` is Markov with kernel `Q`, and its finite-dimensional laws from `x₀` are determined (induction with the Markov property). Conversely the Lean statement is the content of "generator of `T(X)` is `ρΔ - ρ x·∇`" in semigroup form. Lost: (i) the Brownian carrier (the old pathwise statement produced a Brownian `β` on the same space with `T(X_t) = T x₀ - ρ∫ T(X_s) ds + √(2ρ) β_t`), which the manuscript never asserts; (ii) the Lean statement does not itself state "Markov process", but that is implied as just explained and is not needed.
- Use in the proof of Theorem 1.1 (lines ~960-975): only the time-`t` marginal is used: `Z_t = T(X_t) ~ N(T(x₀)e^{-ρt}, (1-e^{-2ρt})I)` from `x₀` (Mehler formula), `T(Y_t) ~ γ_{k₁}` for `Y₀ ~ μ` (obtained by integrating the marginal over `μ`, or directly invariance), the contraction `W₂(T X_t, T Y_t) ≤ √ρ W₂(X_t, Y_t)` (a coupling at the fixed time `t`) and the Gaussian `W₂` formula. No path-space statement is used. So the law form is exactly what the manuscript uses.
- Hypotheses vs old: old `IsLangevin V B P X` (C², probability, Brownian, SDE solutions) + `RigidCurvature` + `IsEigenbasis`; new `IsEuclideanLangevin` (C^∞, probability, kernel) + same two. No hypothesis silently dropped; `T` is an arbitrary orthonormal basis of `E₁` (`IsEigenbasis`), i.e. `T ∈ 𝕊_{F₁}` up to the normalization used by the manuscript (orthonormal in `L²(μ)`, so rows of norm `√ρ`), as before. For `d = 0`, `RigidCurvature` fails (`sInf ∅ = 0`), vacuous and harmless. `firstEigenDim` is a `finrank` (junk `0` if infinite-dimensional): under `RigidCurvature` the space is finite-dimensional (proved elsewhere), and for the quantifier `T : Fin (firstEigenDim V) → …` with `hT : IsEigenbasis` a junk `0` could only make the statement trivial, not false.
- Deviations: (a) C^∞ amendment (low); (b) law form instead of pathwise `β` (low; the stronger old statement implied it; the weaker one is what the manuscript uses).

## 5. `normalization_cutoff`: faithful

Hypotheses: `α n > 0` only; `Pt n` arbitrary families of kernels (no Markov or semigroup assumption), as old `X` was arbitrary. Time-changed kernels `Ptα n u = Pt n ((α n).toNNReal * u)`. This is `X^{(α)}_t = X_{αt}`, manuscript lines 825-835 (cutoff at `t_*` iff cutoff at `t_*/α`; the `SDE` statement `dX^{(α)} = -α∇V dt + √(2α) dB` is the separate `normalization_sde`, unchanged). Conclusions: equivalence of `HasCutoff` for every `δ, t, before, after`; mixing time `t₀/ofReal α` in `ℝ≥0∞` (`α > 0`, so no division-by-zero junk; `⊤/ofReal α = ⊤` correct); product condition `α κ t₀^{(α)} → ∞ ⟺ κ t₀ → ∞` with `toReal`. Convention check on `toNNReal`: `(α.toNNReal * (t/α).toNNReal) = t.toNNReal` for all real `t` (both `0` when `t < 0`), so negative-time junk is consistent on both sides. The statement is pure reparametrization, true for arbitrary kernels, and does not need the diffusion axioms; it is proved in `Extra/EuclideanKernelStatements.lean` as such. Non-vacuity: any family of kernels. The statement says nothing about the generator of `Ptα` being `α𝓛` (that is `normalization_sde`), same as the old split. Faithful.

## 6. `temperature_cutoff`: faithful with remark

Manuscript lines 838-860: `dY = -∇V dt + √(2σ²) dB`, generator `σ²Δ - ∇V·∇`, `CD(ρ,∞) ⟺ Hess V ≥ ρ I` free of `σ`; in the rigid case `ρ = λ₁` the cutoff critical time is free of `σ`.
- Hypotheses vs old: old `hV : C²`, `hY : IsStdBrownian ∧ SDE solutions with a = 1, s = √2 σ` (n > 0), `hp : probability gibbs (V n/σ²)`; new `hY : IsSigmaLangevin (V n) σ (Pt n)` (C^∞, same probability hypothesis, σ-diffusion kernel). `hσ`, `hρ`, `hHess`, `hgap`, `hliminf`, `hc` identical to old. `hgap : ρ n = σ² spectralGap (V n/σ²)`: this is `λ₁(L_σ)` since `L_σ = σ² 𝓛_{V/σ²}` and the Dirichlet form `∫σ²|∇f|² dμ_σ` gives `λ₁(L_σ) = σ² λ₁(V/σ²)`; so `hgap` says "rigid", `ρ n = λ₁(L_σ)` (equality case of the Bakry-Émery bound, `Hess V ≥ ρ n`, hence `Hess(V/σ²) ≥ ρ n/σ² = λ₁(V/σ²)`), as in the manuscript.
- Conclusion: `worstK (Pt n) (meanBall (V n/σ²) c) (δ)` with `δ` evaluated against `gibbs (V n/σ²)` (the invariant law `μ_σ`), at `t n = criticalTime n (ρ n) = log n/(2 ρ n)` with `ρ n = λ₁(L_σ)`: this is the critical time for `L_σ`; it is free of `σ` in the sense that the manuscript states it (it depends on `V` through `ρ n` and not otherwise on `σ`; the identity `ρ n = σ² λ₁(V/σ²)` is the rigid hypothesis). `meanBall (V/σ²) c` is the ball around the mean of `μ_σ`. Fisher, entropy, TV under `ρ =o log`, with the same limits `⊤ → 0`, `⊤ → 0`, `1 → 0` as Corollary 1.4. Cross-check with the proved reduction to `IsRigidSequenceK (V n/σ²) (fun u => Pt n (u/σ²))`: `spectralGap (V n/σ²) = ρ n/σ²`, `Hess(V/σ²) ≥ ρ n/σ²`, `liminf ρ n/σ² > 0`; the cutoff time of the reduced problem is `log n/(2 ρ n/σ²)` in time `u = σ² t`, i.e. `log n/(2 ρ n)` in the original time: consistent.
- Non-vacuity: `σ = 1`, `V n = gaussPot n ρ` (then `hgap`: `ρ = spectralGap`, `hHess`, `hliminf`); for other `σ` take `V n = σ² gaussPot n ρ'` and `Pt n t = Q_n(σ² t)` (hand-checked, §0). Note that `ρ n` is determined by `hgap` and `hHess` is automatic from it plus rigidity; no contradiction.
- Deviations: (a) C^∞ amendment (flagged); (b) `hρ n > 0` and `hgap` are the same flagged "rigid" reading as before. No silent change.
